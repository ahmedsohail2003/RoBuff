UB Hacking 2026 · Deep space

Pick up a space rock with your bare hand, using only friction, after a lift test passes. with your bare hand.

A signal takes 2.56 s to reach the Moon and come back. In a 2024 University of Bristol study, people steering a robot arm with delays up to 2.6 s did worse on every measure. RoBuff lets you steer a simulated robot arm with your hand through a webcam, and switch on the Moon's delay. A see-through ghost arm then shows where the arm is heading before the delayed view comes back. The jaw holds objects by friction alone. It works with a real hand on a laptop webcam. In 5 of 5 test runs steered by code, it lifted a test cube 78.4 mm.

How your hand controls the arm.
Find the hand, turn it into a command, move the arm.

The hand tracking, the arm and the physics all run in your browser. The arm is a simulated SO-ARM100, a small open-source robot arm. No real robot is attached. In Moon mode, your commands take 1.28 s to reach the arm. The picture takes another 1.28 s to come back, as on a real Earth-to-Moon link. A see-through ghost arm shows where the arm is heading. I have not measured yet whether the ghost helps people.

02 · Understand

Turn points into a steady command

One Euro filter

smooths the position of your palm. When your hand is still, it smooths a lot, so the arm shakes less. When your hand moves, it smooths less, so the arm lags less.

  • Pinch to close the jaw, and open your fingers to let go
  • If the camera loses your hand, the arm holds still and keeps its grip
  • The point the arm aims for moves at most 0.25 m/s, so a sudden jerk cannot fling the arm

02

Ask for an object

You type what you want. Gemini, Google's AI model, turns your words into a description the app can build. Without Gemini, a simple keyword reader in the app does this instead. The app builds a 3D model. For things like a mug or a plane, Gemini is asked for 3 to 8 coloured simple shapes. Before it appears, 7 checks decide whether the arm could hold it.

Objects I asked for, and what happened.
Every refusal shows the numbers behind it.

A generated object is only useful if the arm can hold it. So the app checks every request first. Then it places the object and runs a simulated lift test. If the lift fails, the object is removed. I tested seven requests in the browser. Five were lifted, one of them after being rebuilt as a smaller model. The checks refused the other two before they were placed.

Adding a new object to the scene took 210.8 to 258.5 ms. Each lift test took 399 to 551 ms. Both were timed in an automated Chrome test on my laptop, with MuJoCo running in the browser on the CPU.

Built from coloured parts

A mug or a plane is hard to make from one shape. So Gemini is asked for 3 to 8 coloured simple shapes, and the app builds the object from them. Sometimes Gemini sends back one plain shape instead. In the physics engine, each part is its own solid, so the jaw grips the real parts, not one box around the whole object.

In one live run, all 5 requests were scaled down to fit the jaw and passed the lift test. Four of them are below.

Skins

You can also give any object a skin, a painted look such as brushed gold. Nano Banana, Google's image model, draws it from your words. A skin changes only the look, never the physics.

The tools I used. What each one is, and why I needed it.

MuJoCo

Working

A physics engine that works out contact and friction. I run it in the browser so a rock rises only if friction really holds it.

MediaPipe Hand Landmarker

Working · tried with a real hand

Google's hand-tracking model. It finds 21 points on a hand in each camera frame. It runs on your own computer, so no video is uploaded. In a test that fed it a photo of a hand, it took a median 11 ms per frame.

SO-ARM100 from MuJoCo Menagerie

Working

A small open-source robot arm with 5 joints and a jaw. The model comes from MuJoCo Menagerie, a library of checked robot models. I used it as published, with no changes.

Playwright and Vitest

Working

These are automated test tools. Playwright drives a real Chrome browser from a script. Anyone can use it to re-run my tests of the pick, the hand tracking and the object requests. Vitest tests the physics, the filters and the checks without a browser. 381 of 381 unit tests passed.

Gemini 3.8 Flash

Working (format check 5 / 5)

Google's AI model. It turns a typed request into an object description (kind, size, material) that the checks can test. In a live test, 5 of 5 replies came back in the right format. The median reply took 3867 ms. That test only checked the format. The sizes it returned did not always match the request. It also writes the part lists for objects made of parts, and it answers when you talk to RoBuff.

The ghost arm (a predictive display)

Built · result not measured

A second simulation runs your commands with no delay. It draws a see-through arm where the delayed arm is heading. DLR used the same idea on its ROTEX robot arm in space in 1993. Whether it helps people is not measured yet.

Speech recognition in Chrome

Working · needs the internet

Chrome's built-in speech recognition. It turns what you say into text, so you can give commands without typing. Hold V to talk, or press Talk live to talk hands-free. Commands like "stop" or "move left 5 centimetres" are handled in the app. Anything else goes to Gemini when it is connected.

ElevenLabs

Working · with a fallback

A text-to-speech service. It reads RoBuff's replies out loud. In a live check, 5 of 5 test lines came back as audio, and the first audio arrived after a median 176 ms. Without it, the browser's own voice speaks. Captions are always on.

Nano Banana

Working · paid per new image

Google's image model. It paints an object from a few words, such as brushed gold. It uses Nano Banana Pro by default, and Nano Banana 2 Lite is the faster choice. In live runs, a new picture took 12.1 to 12.9 s on Pro and 3.1 to 4.5 s on Lite. Each new picture is paid for, so the app allows up to 25 Pro or 100 Lite pictures a day. The app saves each picture, so the same request is not paid for twice.

Ghost resultNot measured yetNo trials with a person are recorded yet. The tool to record them is built. I wrote the pass rule before collecting any data. With the ghost on, the median task time must be at most 0.75 of the time without it. It must also succeed at least as often. I will be the only person tested, so the result will not show that it helps other people.

Objects can be 10 to 70 mm wide and up to 300 g. I set those limits with a safety margin from grasp tests on the simulated arm. When you steer by hand, the limits are tighter. Objects must be 15 to 55 mm wide and at most 200 g. These are estimates. I have not tested them with a live hand yet. The calculator and the refusals use these tighter limits. I have not checked how much a real SO-ARM100 can lift.
